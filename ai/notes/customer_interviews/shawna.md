## Raw Q/A

**Q: Making good habits (1–10):**
7

**Q: Breaking bad habits (1–10):**
5

**Q: Why those ratings?**
> I'm pretty good at building habits when I'm excited about them or when they're tied to something I care about, like my health or showing up for my clients. But breaking habits is harder for me because it's usually tied to stress. When things get chaotic with the studios, I fall back into patterns like overcommitting, staying up too late, or letting my brain run all night thinking about cancellations and pricing and all that. I can start strong and can stay strong, but I can lose sight of the main goal when life gets busy.

---

**Q: When you have a habit you want to make or break, what do you do? What's your process?**
> Honestly it starts with me deciding I want to feel different. Like, I'll hit a point where I'm like okay, I'm tired of feeling scattered, or I'm tired of feeling like I'm not fully in control of my day. Then I try to set a simple goal, but I usually do too much at first. I'll say I'm going to wake up early, journal, meal prep, stop being on my phone at night, and be more present with my family… all at once.
>
> What works best for me is when I pick one thing and tie it to my routine. Like if I already go to the studio in the morning, I'll stack something onto that. But if it's not attached to something real, I forget. The problem is I'm running a business, and I'm also trying to be a person outside of it, and those two worlds fight for space. That's where I start feeling like I lose my peace.

*[Interviewer pain point note in transcript]:* I do not feel like I have a consistent system for building habits outside of work. I can keep it together at the studios because it's structured. But at home, I can feel scattered.

---

**Q: Have you ever tried a habit app?**
> Yeah. I've tried Noom, Habitify, and Onrise. I still use Noom sometimes for food just because it keeps me honest.

---

**Q: If so, what did you like about it? What didn't you like? Why did you stop using it?**
> What I liked: I liked having something that made me feel like I had a plan. Especially Noom. It's easy, it keeps me aware, and it feels simple enough that I actually stick with it longer.
>
> What I didn't like: Most habit apps feel like they're built for someone who has tons of time to sit and track things. I don't. I'm running two studios, I'm managing staff, and cancellations happen constantly. So I'll be doing great for like 10 days and then something stressful hits and I just stop opening it.
>
> Also, a lot of them just track. They don't help me understand why I keep falling off. Like if I fail a habit, I don't want an app to basically be like "you failed." I want it to help me fix it.

*[Interviewer pain point note in transcript]:* I don't need another checklist. I need something that helps me adjust when life gets messy, because life always gets messy.

---

**Q: Have you read books like Atomic Habits or other productivity content? How difficult is it to apply what you learned?**
> Yes. I've read Atomic Habits, and I love that stuff. I actually believe in it. It's not that I don't understand what to do.
>
> It's applying it consistently that's hard. Like I'll read something and feel super motivated, and then I have two rough days at the studio — maybe instructors cancel last minute or we have a bad week of attendance — and suddenly I'm back to survival mode. And when I'm in survival mode, habits are the first thing to go.
>
> So it's not hard to understand. It's hard to stay steady when the business throws curveballs.

---

**Q: Let's say there was an app that was really effective at helping you establish good habits. What would it look like?**
> I would want it to feel like a coach, but not an annoying one. Like something that understands me and my life, not a generic tracker.
>
> Here's what I'd want:
> - It helps me pick habits that actually match my life, not just perfect-life habits
> - It adjusts when my schedule changes
> - It helps me stay balanced, not just productive
> - It shows me patterns, like "you always fall off when cancellations spike" or "you sleep worse when you work late three nights in a row"
> - It gives me one small thing to focus on, not 10
> - And honestly, it would need to feel peaceful. I'm trying to build peace and identity, not just hustle.
>
> If I opened it and it felt like pressure or guilt, I wouldn't use it. If it felt like clarity, I would.

*[Interviewer pricing note in transcript]:* I'd pay for something like that, probably up to around $10 a week if I could genuinely feel it working.

## Signals

### Supports Our Core Hypothesis
*(HabitDx hypothesis: people fail habits not from lack of motivation or knowledge, but because they can't diagnose *why* they fail and adapt accordingly)*

- **Confirms the failure-pattern problem:** Shawna explicitly wants the app to explain *why* she falls off — "I want it to help me fix it" — not just log failure. This is the exact gap HabitDx's failure profiling targets.
- **Confirms stress as primary failure trigger:** Unpredictable business events (cancellations, staff issues) reliably break her habit streaks — supporting the "external disruption" failure mode we model.
- **Confirms habit stacking as preferred strategy:** She independently describes habit stacking as what works best for her, validating our habit stack generation approach.
- **Confirms pattern-recognition as high value:** She specifically requests pattern insight ("you always fall off when cancellations spike") — a direct analog to HabitDx's weekly iteration and AI analysis features.
- **Confirms willingness to pay:** ~$10/week indicates strong perceived value potential for a solution that *works*.
- **Confirms emotional framing matters:** "Clarity" vs. "pressure or guilt" — the product's tone and framing are as important as its features.

### Threatens Our Core Hypothesis

- **She has residual app usage (Noom):** She still uses Noom for food tracking, suggesting simple checklist apps *do* work for sufficiently contained domains. HabitDx needs to be clearly differentiated beyond the food/health niche.
- **Her primary pain may be workload, not app design:** The root cause of her habit failures could be an objectively unsustainable schedule (running two studios, managing staff). No app may fully solve a structural life problem.
- **$10/week is conditional:** She qualifies the price with "if I could genuinely feel it working" — retention and perceived ROI will be critical; she won't pay for long if progress feels invisible.
